## 1. Post-D42 Dependency Analysis

D42 establishes the governance boundary for mathematical sources, references, canonical designations, domain-expert review, citation, provenance, and bounded source use. It protects mathematical authority from being created by publication, citation, institutional adoption, expert status, AI output, provider trust, or repeated use.

The chain now governs mathematical context and source authority, but a distinct educational-authority gap remains. The engine may use mathematically grounded knowledge and authorised content to propose learning responses, yet no locked decision fully governs **what a learning experience is intended to accomplish, what an outcome claim means, who may authorise an outcome claim, how learner-facing wording relates to mathematical truth and learner state, and how intended outcomes remain separate from achieved outcomes**.

D3/D4 govern curriculum structure and progression; D5/D7 govern content and experience lifecycle; D22/D27 govern authoritative learner conclusions and state commitments; D28 governs pedagogical adaptation and learning response; D29 governs delivery and interaction. None of these decisions establishes a general authority model for pedagogical intent and outcome claims. The single highest-priority unresolved governance boundary is therefore **Pedagogical Intent, Outcome & Learner-Facing Claim Authority**.

| Locked decision | What it resolves | Remaining dependency |
|---|---|---|
| **D3/D4 — Curriculum and Academic Progression** | Educational structure and progression are not mathematical truth or automatic learner capability. | Authority and meaning of intended outcomes and educational claims. |
| **D5/D7 — Content and Experience Lifecycle** | Content/experience definition, offer, instance, delivery, participation, and completion remain distinct. | What an experience is intended to accomplish and how that intent is represented. |
| **D8/D21/D26 — Assessment, Evidence, Interpretation** | Observation, evidence, interpretation, and misconception remain bounded. | Whether and how outcome claims describe learner achievement without becoming automatic state. |
| **D22/D27 — Conclusions and State** | State claims/commitments require explicit authority and cannot arise automatically. | Educational outcome semantics before any claim is considered for state. |
| **D28/D29 — Adaptation and Delivery** | Adaptation/response and delivery do not create learning or state. | Separation of pedagogical intent, learner-facing claims, and delivered wording. |
| **D40–D42 — Mathematical Claim/Context/Source Authority** | Mathematical truth, context, source, and review are explicit. | How educational goals and learner-facing claims depend on, but do not alter, mathematical authority. |

This is the next priority because without D43, a content author, curriculum, policy, assessment, adaptation, AI system, provider, or delivery client could silently convert an intended lesson objective into an achieved learner outcome, or convert learner-facing wording into a claim about capability, mastery, readiness, misconception, or progression. That would collapse pedagogy, assessment, state, and learner consent at the point where the engine communicates purpose and outcome.

D43 does not define a curriculum, learning taxonomy, assessment rubric, mastery standard, grading system, or pedagogical method. It defines the authority boundary needed to keep intent, expected outcome, delivered response, observed evidence, interpreted result, authoritative conclusion, and learner state separate.

## 2. Purpose

D43 defines the authority semantics for pedagogical intent, learning objectives, expected outcomes, learner-facing claims, completion criteria, outcome interpretation, claim status, and the relationship between intended and achieved outcomes.

> **An intended outcome is not an achieved outcome. A delivered explanation is not evidence of learning. A learner-facing claim is not learner consent, mathematical truth, or authoritative learner state.**

D43 ensures that educational purpose and outcome language are explicit, scoped, provenance-linked, reviewable, and non-authoritative unless a separate locked authority permits a specific conclusion or state commitment.

## 3. Scope

D43 governs pedagogical intent and outcome claims associated with domains, content, curriculum, learning experiences, offers, responses, delivery, assessment, evidence, interpretations, conclusions, and learner records.

| Within D43 | Outside D43 |
|---|---|
| Intent, objective, expected outcome, learner-facing claim, completion criterion, achieved-outcome claim, scope, and lifecycle | Curriculum design, pedagogical method selection, assessment scoring, AI, content authoring, mathematical validation, UI/API, or implementation |
| Separation of intent, delivery, evidence, conclusion, and state | Declaring mastery/readiness/progression/misconception standards or grading authority |
| Claim provenance, status, review, correction, conflict, and historical protection | Specific learning taxonomy, rubric, instructional sequence, learner model, or client experience |
| Relationship between educational claims and mathematical/content authority | Persistence, schemas, delivery runtime, provider integration, or governance tooling |

D43 creates no specific objective, outcome, achievement standard, or learner claim.

## 4. Authority Model

**Pedagogical intent** is an explicit statement of what an authorised learning experience, content asset, adaptation, or response is designed to support, explain, practise, or make available. Intent describes purpose; it does not describe what the learner has achieved.

An **expected outcome** is a bounded statement of a possible learner capability, understanding, action, or evidence pattern associated with an intent. It is prospective and conditional, not a prediction or learner fact.

A **learner-facing claim** is wording presented or made available to a learner about content, purpose, expectations, progress, evidence, interpretation, conclusion, or state. Its audience-facing form does not change its authority category.

An **achieved-outcome claim** is a claim about what a learner has demonstrated or attained. It requires D8/D21/D26/D27/D22 authority and must not be inferred from intent, delivery, participation, completion, self-report, or system confidence.

| Concept | Meaning | It does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** | Purpose an experience/content/response is designed to support. | Learner achievement, consent, or state. |
| **Objective** | Bounded educational aim. | Mathematical truth or guaranteed result. |
| **Expected outcome** | Prospective capability/evidence possibility. | Prediction, learner fact, mastery, or readiness. |
| **Learner-facing claim** | Claim communicated or exposed to a learner. | Truth merely because it was communicated. |
| **Completion criterion** | Defined condition for experience/lifecycle completion. | Learning, state, or achieved outcome. |
| **Observed outcome** | Evidence-bound observation of learner action/response. | Interpretation or authoritative conclusion. |
| **Achieved-outcome claim** | Governed claim about learner demonstration/attainment. | Automatic state or universal capability. |
| **Pedagogical suitability** | Fit for a purpose/learner/context. | Mathematical correctness or learner consent. |
| **Outcome status** | Proposed, expected, observed, interpreted, authorised, contested, or other explicit status. | Authority from metadata alone. |

## 5. Intent and Outcome Separation

Every educational experience or response that states an objective or outcome must distinguish its intent, expected outcome, completion condition, delivered content, learner action, observed evidence, interpretation, achieved-outcome claim, and state effect.

| Layer | Governing question | Authority limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** | What is this designed to support? | Does not describe the learner. |
| **Expectation** | What might be demonstrated under conditions? | Does not predict or establish achievement. |
| **Completion** | What lifecycle condition was met? | Does not establish learning. |
| **Delivery** | What was presented or realised? | Does not establish reception or understanding. |
| **Participation** | What interaction/delivery fact occurred? | Does not establish evidence sufficiency. |
| **Observation/evidence** | What qualified learner-related fact is recorded? | Does not establish interpretation or state. |
| **Interpretation** | What bounded meaning is proposed/reviewed? | Does not automatically become learner truth. |
| **Achieved outcome** | What authorised claim, if any, is made? | Requires separate authority; not automatic state. |
| **State commitment** | What prospective learner-state effect is authorised? | D22/D27/D30 conditions remain required. |

No layer may silently stand in for another.

## 6. Learner-Facing Claims

Learner-facing wording must preserve whether it expresses intent, expectation, instruction, question, possibility, observation, interpretation, conclusion, or state. A simplified statement may be pedagogically appropriate under D28/D29 while remaining distinct from a mathematical claim under D40/D41 and from an achieved-outcome claim under D22/D27.

The fact that a learner sees, hears, acknowledges, repeats, selects, or responds to wording does not establish that the wording was understood, accepted, consented to, or achieved. A statement such as “you can,” “you understand,” “you mastered,” “you are ready,” or “you have a misconception” is an achieved-outcome or state-related claim requiring appropriate authority; it must not be generated from intent, delivery, completion, or a weak observation alone.

## 7. Outcome Authority and Evidence Boundary

D8/D21/D26/D27/D22 remain controlling. An expected outcome may define what evidence could be relevant, but it does not make any observation sufficient. Completion criteria may define lifecycle completion, but they do not establish learning. A learner-facing progress statement may be offered as a proposal or bounded interpretation, but it is not authoritative state without explicit authority.

Where an achieved-outcome claim is considered, it must identify the qualified evidence, purpose, formal/mathematical context, assessment/interpretation basis, authority, scope, uncertainty, conflict, effective period, and prospective status. A claim may be authorised for one narrow purpose without becoming universal learner capability.

## 8. Pedagogical Adaptation and Delivery Boundary

D28/D29 remain controlling. An adaptation may choose or propose a response in relation to intent and expected outcomes, but it cannot claim that the learner achieved the outcome. Delivery may realise a response, but it cannot create learning, evidence, consent, interpretation, conclusion, or state.

A client/provider may render or phrase an intent/outcome claim only within the semantic authority supplied by the engine. It may not strengthen “may support” into “has achieved,” collapse uncertainty, or omit a material limitation in a way that changes authority.

## 9. Mathematical and Content Boundary

D40–D42 remain controlling. Pedagogical intent and learner-facing claims may refer to mathematical content, but pedagogical usefulness, curriculum placement, source citation, expert review, or content publication cannot create mathematical truth. Mathematical admissibility does not automatically make an educational outcome appropriate or achieved.

A learning objective may simplify mathematics for a learner-facing context, but any simplification must preserve the formal context and limitations required by D41/D42. If the underlying mathematical claim or context is unresolved, dependent intent/outcome use must be constrained or fail closed.

## 10. Versioning, Lifecycle, and Correction

Intent and outcome definitions require identity, version, context, purpose, authorising authority, scope, assumptions, evidence expectations, completion relation, and effective period where relevant. A revised objective or outcome is not automatically equivalent to an earlier one.

Corrections, withdrawals, supersessions, and changed expectations are prospective and additive. They must not rewrite historical experience definitions, offers, delivery, completion, observations, interpretations, achieved-outcome claims, decisions, state, choices, or provenance.

If a change affects the meaning of an outcome or the evidence relevant to it, future applicability must be reviewed under D25/D31/D38/D39. Historical claims remain linked to the objective/outcome version applicable when they were made.

## 11. Conflict and Fail-Closed Rules

Outcome conflicts may concern intent, expectation, completion, mathematical context, content, curriculum, assessment, evidence, interpretation, state, learner-facing wording, policy, provider, client, or historical records. Conflict creates no achieved outcome, learner state, or authority to choose a flattering interpretation.

No precedence may be inferred from completion, delivery, participation, learner acknowledgement, client wording, curriculum position, AI confidence, provider reputation, source popularity, test success, or operational convenience.

| Unresolved condition | Safe constrained outcome |
|---|---|
| Intent/outcome meaning, scope, or version is ambiguous | Do not make a consequential achieved-outcome claim. |
| Expected outcome lacks evidence basis or authority | Keep it prospective; no learner-state inference. |
| Delivery/completion is being used as achievement | Preserve lifecycle facts; reject the achievement inference. |
| Learner-facing wording strengthens or changes authority | Use bounded wording or block the claim. |
| Qualified evidence/interpretation is conflicting or insufficient | Preserve uncertainty; no achieved-outcome/state effect. |
| Mathematical/content context is unresolved | Constrain dependent educational claim/use. |
| Outcome version/equivalence is unresolved | No substitution or historical reinterpretation. |
| Representative, provider, AI, or client authority is unclear | No consequential outcome claim. |

Fail-closed behaviour must not create learner achievement, mastery, readiness, progression, misconception, consent, choice, mathematical truth, or state.

## 12. Historical Protection

D43 must never rewrite, delete, conceal, or retroactively relabel intent, objective, expected outcome, completion criterion, delivery, participation, learner-facing wording, evidence, interpretation, achieved-outcome claim, conclusion, state, choice, decision, provider exchange, or provenance.

A later correction, outcome change, interpretation, assessment result, state decision, or pedagogical revision affects future use only through explicit authority. It cannot make a historical delivery, completion, learner response, claim, or state effect not have occurred.

## 14. Prohibited Behaviours

14.1. Treating an intent, objective, expected outcome, completion criterion, delivery, participation, acknowledgement, or learner-facing statement as proof of achievement, learning, mastery, readiness, progression, misconception, or state.

14.2. Presenting a prospective expectation as a fact about the learner.

14.3. Strengthening or weakening learner-facing wording in a way that changes its authority, uncertainty, scope, or status.

14.4. Treating pedagogical usefulness, curriculum placement, mathematical validity, source citation, expert review, publication, or policy activation as achieved learner outcome authority.

14.5. Allowing AI, provider, client, assessment, adaptation, delivery, or implementation to assert achievement or state without D8/D21/D26/D27/D22 authority.

14.6. Treating completion, delivery, interaction, acknowledgement, repetition, silence, or continuation as learner consent, understanding, evidence sufficiency, or achievement.

14.7. Silently changing outcome definitions, conditions, formal context, evidence expectations, versions, or applicability to make an outcome appear achieved.

14.8. Resolving conflicting intent, outcome, evidence, interpretation, mathematical context, policy, provider, or learner-facing claims through recency, confidence, convenience, or flattering interpretation.

14.9. Rewriting historical objectives, outcomes, delivery, evidence, claims, conclusions, state, choices, decisions, or provenance after a pedagogical or assessment change.

14.10. Implementing pedagogical outcome, learner-claim, assessment, state, AI, delivery, persistence, UI/API, or any other functionality from D43.

## 15. Explicit Deferrals

D43 deliberately leaves the following matters unresolved:

| Deferred matter | Reason for deferral |
|---|---|
| Specific learning taxonomy, objectives, outcomes, pedagogical methods, instructional sequence, or curriculum standards | D43 governs authority distinctions without selecting pedagogy or curriculum. |
| Mastery, readiness, progression, misconception, grading, certification, ranking, or competence criteria | D2/D8/D22/D27 constrain them; no substantive standard is invented. |
| Exact assessment rubrics, evidence sufficiency, outcome thresholds, and review bodies | These require future purpose- and domain-specific governance. |
| Learner-facing wording standards, accessibility language, translation, tone, and client presentation | D29 constrains semantic delivery; presentation rules remain deferred. |
| Specific mathematical sources, formal contexts, content, policy, AI, providers, identity, data, and implementation | D1–D42 remain controlling; D43 creates no additional authority. |
| Legal, institutional, examination, accreditation, or jurisdictional outcome authority | D43 is not a legal or institutional standard. |
| Slice 6 scope and implementation authorisation | D43 is a governance boundary, not implementation approval. |

## 16. Required Contract Changes, if Any

**No contract changes are required or authorised by D43 at this stage.**

If D43 is approved and a later controlled implementation is explicitly authorised, future contracts may need distinct representations for intent, objective, expected outcome, completion criterion, delivered response, participation, learner-facing claim, observation, evidence, interpretation, achieved-outcome claim, conclusion, state commitment, authority, purpose, scope, version, formal context, uncertainty, conflict, status, effective period, and provenance.

Future contracts must not encode completion as achievement, expectation as learner fact, learner-facing wording as consent, mathematical validity as educational outcome, or delivery as learning. They must preserve prospective/historical distinctions and fail closed when outcome meaning, evidence, authority, context, version, or conflict is unresolved. This is impact analysis only and does not authorise contract, code, schema, test, persistence, pedagogy, assessment, AI, UI/API, or repository changes.

# D28 — Pedagogical Adaptation & Learning-Response Authority

> **D28 — PROPOSED / HUMAN REVIEW ONLY**
>
> This is a governance-specification-only document. D1–D27 are preserved exactly as approved and locked. D28 authorises no code, contract, repository, schema, migration, persistence, assessment, AI, UI/API, D29, or Slice 6 work.

## 1. Post-D27 Dependency Analysis

D27 closes the authority boundary for state semantics, authoritative state claims, state commitments, prospective effectiveness, and execution. It deliberately does not decide what a learning engine may do **with** an authorised state or a qualified evidence/conclusion context when forming a pedagogical response, adapting a candidate experience, or presenting a new learning opportunity.

The next highest-priority unresolved boundary is therefore **Pedagogical Adaptation & Learning-Response Authority**. This boundary governs how the engine may propose or authorise a pedagogical adaptation or learning response while preserving learner choice, current experience authority, content/curriculum/policy authority, delivery constraints, evidence/state distinctions, and fail-closed behaviour.

| Locked decision | What it resolves | Remaining dependency |
|---|---|---|
| **D1 — Learner Choice** | Only explicit authorised choice creates the relevant learner commitment. | Whether a pedagogical response may be proposed/offered and what may never be inferred as acceptance. |
| **D7/D23 — Experience Lifecycle and Continuity** | Experience facts, interruption, resumption, and current executability remain distinct. | How a current pedagogical response relates to an existing/new experience without silent mutation or continuation. |
| **D9/D11 — Decisioning and Policy** | Decisioning executes authorised policy over qualified inputs; policy lifecycle is explicit. | The pedagogical subject matter and authority that a policy/engine may legitimately govern. |
| **D22/D27 — Conclusions, State, and State Commitments** | State authority and commitments have distinct approval/effectiveness/execution. | How state/evidence may inform a learning response without becoming an automatic pedagogical or learner-choice command. |
| **D26 — Semantic Interpretation** | Interpretations/misconception hypotheses remain derived and non-authoritative by default. | How, if at all, such interpretations may inform a bounded response without converting a hypothesis into a route or diagnosis. |
| **D5/D10/D25 — Content, Activation, Policy Relationships** | Content and policy authority/activation/relationships are explicit. | How adaptations select only current authorised content/experience/policy, without substitution or activation by computation. |

This is the next priority because the engine’s core purpose is not merely to store evidence or state; it is to form learning decisions and responses. Without D28, implementation risks making a computed candidate, state value, misconception hypothesis, policy result, completed experience, or delivery capability into an automatic pedagogical action, offer, redirection, or learner commitment.

Other unresolved matters remain—especially substantive mastery/readiness/progression/misconception authority, assessment scoring, active-session mechanics, legal/operational privacy, policy runtime, and migration—but D28 is the direct boundary needed before the engine may safely use the governed context to form a pedagogical response.

## 2. Purpose

D28 defines the authority semantics for **pedagogical adaptation** and **learning response**. It distinguishes a proposed adaptation, an authorised adaptation, a learning response, a learning offer, current decisioning, delivery, learner choice, and any resulting conclusion/state commitment.

> **A learning response is not automatically a learning outcome. A pedagogical adaptation is not automatically an offer. An offer is not consent or choice. A policy result is not pedagogical authority. Deterministic computation is not authority.**

D28 permits no automatic learner commitment, state mutation, assessment conclusion, content activation, curriculum progression, or experience mutation merely because an adaptation or response appears pedagogically useful.

## 3. Scope

D28 governs the authority, purpose, scope, lifecycle, provenance, and fail-closed treatment of pedagogical adaptations and learning responses derived from currently permitted context. It governs how an adaptation may be considered, reviewed, approved, made effective, used to form a response, and related to an offer or existing experience.

| Within D28 | Outside D28 |
|---|---|
| Pedagogical adaptation as a bounded proposed/authorised change in learning approach, sequencing, support, representation, or experience choice | Mathematical truth, content truth, curriculum authority, assessment scoring, diagnosis, mastery, readiness, progression, certification, grading, or misconception authority |
| Learning response as a bounded semantic result or governed opportunity produced for a learner context | UI, client, device/session detection, transport, storage, persistence, API, delivery runtime, or interaction mechanics |
| Authority, policy, evidence/state grounding, lifecycle, learner-choice protection, and current-context constraints | AI runtime, identity/authentication, privacy implementation, legal compliance, migration, or state ontology definition |
| Prospective relation of a response to offers/experiences | Automatic learner consent, choice, state mutation, or silent content/policy/curriculum substitution |

## 4. Authority Model

A **pedagogical adaptation** is a bounded proposed or authorised change to a learning approach, sequence, support level, representation, interaction structure, experience selection, or pedagogical route for a stated purpose and context. It may be informed by qualified evidence, interpretations, authoritative state, curriculum context, content/policy constraints, and declared delivery capabilities, but none of these creates adaptation authority by itself.

A **learning response** is a bounded semantic output or governed opportunity that communicates what the engine is permitted to return for a current learner context. It may be safe/non-material, actionless, or material/offer-related only under the existing decisioning and learner-choice rules. It is not itself learning, competence, state, consent, choice, delivery, or execution.

An **adaptation authority** is an explicitly recognised authority permitted to define, approve, activate, or use a named category of pedagogical adaptation for a stated purpose, scope, policy, curriculum/content context, and period. D28 creates no default adaptation authority. D15–D16 govern actor recognition and action; D9–D11 govern decisioning/policy; D5/D10 govern content/curriculum activation; D1 governs learner choice.

| Concept | Meaning | It does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Pedagogical proposal** | A candidate adaptation or response suggested for review/use. | Authority, offer, learner choice, state, or content activation. |
| **Authorised adaptation** | An adaptation approved within explicit authority, policy, purpose, scope, and context. | Automatic learner acceptance, current executability, or learning outcome. |
| **Learning response** | A bounded semantic result/opportunity returned under current decisioning constraints. | Delivery, consent, choice, state, mastery, or completion. |
| **Learning offer** | A D1-governed opportunity that may require explicit learner selection. | Acceptance, execution, state, or preference. |
| **Adaptation execution** | A separately authorised prospective use of an effective adaptation. | Silent experience mutation, policy bypass, or historical rewrite. |
| **Learner choice** | An explicit learner or D24-authorised representative action where D1 permits. | Adaptation authority or learning outcome. |

## 5. Adaptation Categories and Boundaries

D28 does not create substantive pedagogy or content authority, but it distinguishes categories that a future authorised policy may govern. These categories include: sequencing or pacing proposal; support/scaffolding proposal; representation/notation proposal; interaction/response-mode proposal; difficulty/context proposal; experience/offer candidate; and continuity-constrained response to an interruption or changed context.

A category label is descriptive, not authoritative. Each proposed or authorised adaptation must identify its purpose, learner/context scope, relevant content/curriculum/experience, policy, evidence/state/conclusion references where applicable, delivery requirements, uncertainty, limitations, effective period, authority, and permitted consequence.

An adaptation must not assert that a learner needs a particular route merely because an interpretation, state value, error pattern, completion event, or algorithm selected it. An adaptation may be appropriate for a purpose without establishing a learner fact. A pedagogical route is not a diagnosis, a capability claim, a progression decision, or a learner preference.

## 6. Evidence, State, and Context Requirements

A pedagogical adaptation may use only context that is independently authorised for the stated purpose. Where evidence is used, it must be qualified and purpose-sufficient under D21. Where semantic interpretation is used, it must remain within D26. Where an authoritative conclusion or state is used, it must satisfy D22/D27. Privacy/data handling must satisfy D19–D20. Current content, experience, policy, delivery, and version context must satisfy D5/D10/D11/D13/D23/D25.

No upstream input compels an adaptation. In particular:

| Input | Permitted use | Prohibited conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified evidence | May support a bounded pedagogical consideration when purpose permits. | Not automatic need, deficit, route, or state. |
| D26 interpretation/misconception hypothesis | May be a bounded, uncertain proposal input where explicitly permitted. | Not diagnosis, learner truth, or automatic remediation. |
| D22/D27 conclusion/state | May inform a current policy-bound consideration within scope. | Not automatic redirection, offer acceptance, or mutation. |
| Curriculum/content context | May constrain educational structure and available authorised options. | Not learner consent, truth, or adaptation authority. |
| Delivery capability | May constrain response compatibility. | Not permission to silently change pedagogy or experience. |
| Prior lifecycle/participation | May provide historical context. | Not competence, preference, acceptance, or current continuation authority. |
| AI proposal | May be reviewed as a proposal under D14. | Not adaptation authority or learner response authority. |

## 7. Learning-Response Boundary

A learning response may be **safe/non-material** when it is conceptless or bounded as required by the existing decisioning contract, actionless, offerless, non-consensual, and non-mutating. It may communicate that no compatible or authorised material response is available, or provide another constrained semantic outcome permitted by current policy.

A response may be **material** only when it is grounded in the applicable authorised policy, current content/curriculum/experience context, qualified inputs, current delivery capabilities, and current authority. A material response may identify or present a learning opportunity, but it does not create learner commitment. Where D1 requires choice, it must remain an offer pending explicit `select-offer` by the learner or a D24-authorised representative action.

A learning response is not automatically delivery. The delivery layer may only execute a response/offer after current executability is established under D23 and all content, policy, data, authority, capability, and choice conditions remain satisfied. A response cannot silently mutate an existing experience or substitute content, policy, curriculum, version, or route.

## 8. Adaptation and Offer Boundary

An adaptation may be used to form a candidate opportunity only when the candidate is within current authorised content, curriculum, policy, experience, version, and delivery bounds. Candidate generation is not offer authorisation. Offer authorisation is not learner choice. Learner choice is not state or learning.

Where an adaptation changes path, focus, experience identity, or another D1-governed commitment, the result must be an explicit offer and may not be treated as accepted until explicit `select-offer` occurs. Decline, defer, request-alternative, or pause must not be converted into movement toward the original offer; the existing D1 semantics remain controlling.

An adaptation must not be used to bypass an existing interruption, current-executability failure, content withdrawal, policy change, version conflict, evidence conflict, authority restriction, or learner-choice requirement. D23 governs whether an existing instance may continue/resume; D28 may form a new candidate response only under the new current context and applicable authority.

## 9. Lifecycle and Authority Actions

The lifecycle of an adaptation/response remains distinct from policy/content/experience lifecycle and from learner state:

> **proposal → review → approval/rejection → recording → effectiveness → consideration/use → offer/response → learner choice where required → delivery/execution → correction/revocation → staleness → prospective supersession → historical retention**

| Stage | Meaning | No automatic consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Proposal** | A possible adaptation/response is suggested. | No authority, offer, choice, delivery, or state effect. |
| **Review** | An authorised review considers grounding, purpose, scope, alternatives, constraints, and risks. | Review is not approval or correctness. |
| **Approval/rejection** | A recognised authority acts on the exact bounded proposal. | Approval is not current effectiveness, offer acceptance, or execution. |
| **Recording** | The action and provenance are preserved. | Recording is not authority or delivery. |
| **Effectiveness** | The adaptation/response may be used prospectively within current scope. | No learner choice or state effect. |
| **Consideration/use** | A decisioning process considers the effective adaptation. | Consideration is not offer, delivery, or learning. |
| **Offer/response** | A current semantic response or opportunity is produced. | No acceptance or consent. |
| **Learner choice** | Explicit `select-offer`, or D24-authorised representative action where applicable, commits the relevant learner choice. | No automatic learning/state conclusion. |
| **Delivery/execution** | A separately authorised current action delivers/executes the response/experience. | No automatic learning, mastery, or state. |
| **Correction/revocation/staleness/supersession** | Future reliance is changed by an explicit governed action. | No rewriting of history. |

D15–D16 govern who may act and how actions become effective. D28 authorises no actor, policy, content, curriculum, or delivery implementation by itself.

## 10. Current Context and Changed-Context Rules

A pedagogical adaptation is valid only within its declared current context. If content, curriculum, policy, version, experience, evidence, authority, data rights, delivery capability, or learner-choice context changes, the adaptation/response must be re-evaluated prospectively. Historic appropriateness does not establish current applicability.

No current response may silently substitute or alter an existing experience. If an old instance is no longer currently executable under D23, D28 may produce no response, a constrained response, or a new current offer only where current authority/policy/content/curriculum/experience conditions permit it. A new response is not a continuation or migration of the old instance unless D13/D23 explicitly govern that relationship.

A response must not be formed from stale, revoked, restricted, conflicting, or unresolved evidence, interpretation, state, policy, authority, content, curriculum, or version context where the condition is consequential. When current context is unavailable or ambiguous, D28 requires a constrained/no-offer/non-executable result.

## 11. Learner Choice, Consent, and Representation

D1 remains controlling. Adaptation, recommendation, candidate generation, response production, state, conclusion, evidence, interpretation, behaviour, completion, delivery capability, silence, or technical continuation cannot become consent or choice.

Only explicit `select-offer` may authorise an offered path/focus commitment where D1 requires it. Under D24, a representative may perform such a choice only within an explicit, effective, purpose- and scope-bound learner-choice representation. Representative action must remain attributed to the representative and must not be recorded as the learner’s personal action.

A response may be presented or offered without creating consent. Decline, defer, request-alternative, pause, or no response must retain their D1 meanings and must not be used as inferred preference. D28 grants no authority to select for a learner, renew a choice, resume a prior experience, or change a learner commitment silently.

## 12. AI Boundary

D14 remains fully controlling. AI may propose a bounded adaptation, response wording, candidate ordering, or review item only when the task/input scope is independently authorised. AI cannot create adaptation authority, determine a learner’s pedagogical need, diagnose, select an offer, accept an offer, alter state, activate content/policy/curriculum, resume an experience, or resolve authority/conflict.

AI plausibility, confidence, provider trust, successful prior output, or operational acceptance is not pedagogical authority. Human semantic review and policy-permitted acceptance remain distinct from deterministic decisioning and learner choice. AI output must remain visibly distinguishable from the governing policy, human authority, response, offer, choice, delivery, and state.

## 13. Conflict and Fail-Closed Rules

Conflicts may concern adaptation proposals, learner responses, evidence, interpretations, states, content, curriculum, policies, versions, delivery capabilities, experiences, authorities, representatives, or learner choice. Conflict identification creates no adaptation, response, offer, state, or execution authority.

No precedence may be inferred from recency, prior learner behaviour, completion, source popularity, institutional status, technical availability, storage order, policy/version number, AI confidence, provider trust, client capability, convenience, or prior response success. D18 governs consequential unresolved cross-domain conflict.

| Unresolved condition | Safe constrained outcome |
|---|---|
| Adaptation authority, purpose, scope, policy, or provenance is unresolved | Do not approve/effect/use the adaptation consequentially. |
| Input evidence/interpretation/state is insufficient, stale, revoked, conflicting, or out of purpose | Do not form a consequential adaptation or response from it. |
| Current content/curriculum/policy/version/experience context is withdrawn, incompatible, or ambiguous | Do not continue, substitute, migrate, or execute; use current-context decisioning only. |
| Delivery capability is incompatible or unclear | Do not silently downgrade, upgrade, substitute, or change pedagogy. |
| Learner-choice or D24 representation authority is required but absent/unclear | Do not create a commitment or infer choice; produce a constrained/no-offer result. |
| Data/right authority is absent or does not cover the proposed use | Do not access/use/disclose the required information. |
| Competing adaptations/responses/authorities cannot be resolved | Preserve the conflict; no inferred winner, offer, execution, or state effect. |
| Required current or historical context is unavailable/ambiguous | Fail closed under D12/D23; do not reconstruct by similarity or recency. |

Fail-closed behaviour must be non-mutating, non-consensual, non-choice-making, and non-learning-assertive. It must not convert a constrained outcome into decline, failure, disinterest, misconception, or learner-state evidence.

## 14. Historical Protection

D28 must never rewrite, delete, conceal, or retroactively relabel an adaptation proposal, review, approval/rejection, response, offer, learner choice, delivery fact, experience instance, evidence, interpretation, conclusion, state, commitment, event, policy applicability, version relationship, authority record, or provenance.

A later correction, revocation, staleness marking, withdrawal, policy change, content change, context change, or supersession affects future reliance and executability only, through an explicit governed action. It does not change what response was produced, what offer was made, what choice was recorded, what delivery occurred, or what state was historically effective.

## 16. Prohibited Behaviours

16.1. Treating evidence, interpretations, misconception hypotheses, learner state, conclusions, commitments, completion, participation, behaviour, or deterministic computation as automatic pedagogical authority or learner need.

16.2. Treating a pedagogical adaptation, recommendation, response, candidate, offer, or delivery event as learning, competence, mastery, readiness, progression, misconception, consent, choice, or state.

16.3. Automatically redirecting, remediating, reordering, changing difficulty, changing pedagogy, or selecting a route without applicable authority, current context, and the required learner-choice treatment.

16.4. Treating candidate generation as offer authorisation, an offer as acceptance, or a response as delivery/execution.

16.5. Using adaptation/response authority to bypass D1, D23 current executability, D24 representation, D25 policy relationships, D26 interpretation boundaries, D27 state authority, or any other locked constraint.

16.6. Silently substituting, migrating, downgrading, upgrading, continuing, resuming, redirecting, or mutating an experience, content, curriculum, policy, version, or delivery mode.

16.7. Treating interruption, abandonment, completion, delivery availability, technical reconnection, silence, decline, deferment, request-alternative, or learner behaviour as inferred preference, consent, failure, competence, or state.

16.8. Allowing AI confidence, provider trust, institutional status, technical capability, storage custody, prior success, metadata, recency, or convenience to become pedagogical or learner-choice authority.

16.9. Resolving competing adaptations, responses, policies, contexts, authorities, evidence, states, or learner choices through recency, majority, popularity, technical availability, or convenience.

16.10. Using a safe/non-material or constrained response to hide, imply, or manufacture a learner conclusion, preference, state, consent, or choice.

16.11. Rewriting historical responses, offers, choices, experience instances, delivery facts, evidence, interpretations, conclusions, commitments, state, events, authority, policy applicability, or provenance after a later change.

16.12. Implementing pedagogical adaptation, learning response, delivery, policy orchestration, AI, persistence, UI/API, state mutation, or any other functionality from D28.
